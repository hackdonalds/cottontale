# Selection-driven docks beat menus for phone GMs

No surveyed product gives a GM a full table on a phone, so Cottontale has no layout to copy, only mechanisms. Roll20 assigns phones to players and tablets to GMs, Foundry targets a 1366x768 monitor with mouse and keyboard, and the 3D products are desktop-only (TaleSpire) or cancelled (Project Sigil). The mechanisms that the best tools converge on are consistent across all three families studied: act on the selected thing and swap the bottom bar to its actions; ask the user for one coarse gesture and derive the fiddly parts; open on a finished scene instead of an empty one; keep only the current tool, undo and one or two controls permanently visible. Cottontale's audit shows it already owns most of the fast *building* vocabulary (drag-paint, room-drag, scatter, slab copy/paste, per-room lock and reroll) and most of the recommended sheet skeleton. Its four pain points come from what surrounds that vocabulary: nothing placed can be selected, one finger cannot move the camera in Build, the GM has no encounter surface, a 15-row menu holds everything else, and nobody is ever asked whether they are running the game. The redesign should therefore spend its effort on selection, a contextual dock, an encounter sheet and a role-aware first minute, not on new build tools. Evidence strength is uneven: vendor documentation and platform guidelines are solid; phone-specific GM workflows, Reddit sentiment and several named tools (Alchemy, Tabletop Simulator, Inkarnate, Dungeondraft's library, World Anvil's mobile UX) are thin or unsourced, and the report marks which is which.

## The phone-GM seat is empty, and bolt-on phone support rots

### Three ways to split prep from play, none designed for a phone

Virtual tabletops separate building from running in three ways. Roll20, Foundry, Owlbear Rodeo, D&D Beyond Maps and Shmeppy use one always-editable canvas with layers and a tool rail. TaleSpire has an explicit build mode that one or more GMs can enter at any time "to change the world without interrupting play" ([TaleSpire on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/720620/TaleSpire/)). Tabletop Simulator and Tabletop Playground are physics sandboxes with no real split (thin evidence, snippet-only). TaleSpire's model fits a 3D product best, because placing geometry and moving minis need different one-finger behaviour, and its key property is that entering build does not tear down the game in progress. Cottontale has the two modes but not that property: switching to Build deselects, closes sheets and lands back on Move afterwards (audit §2, §12 item 16).

Vendors say plainly that phones are not their GM target. Foundry's requirements read "a monitor no smaller than 1366x768" and "designed for mouse and keyboard" ([Foundry requirements](https://foundryvtt.com/article/requirements/)). Roll20 retired its native app in February 2026 to go "all-in on mobile browser first", but what it ships on phones is sheets and dice, with GMs pointed at tablets ([Roll20 blog, Feb 2026](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/were-retiring-the-roll20-mobile-app-to-build-something-better-heres-why/); the tablet/phone split itself is snippet-only from [Roll20's mobile page](https://pages.roll20.net/dnd-roll20-mobile-tools)). D&D Beyond said Maps "isn't going to be initially optimized for phone and tablet" ([Maps launch guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1570-d-d-beyond-maps-how-to-start-playing-today)). The Foundry module ecosystem is the best record of what phone users miss, and its lesson is durability: a 2021 guide's phone modules no longer work on newer Foundry, and Mobile Token Movement was last updated four years ago ([Foundry Hub in-person guide](https://foundryvtt-hub.com/guide/using-foundry-for-in-person-gaming); [Mobile Token Movement](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/mobile-token-movement)). First-party phone support is the durable advantage, and heat, load time and frame rate are named by users next to layout ([Swipe VTT](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/swipe-vtt)), which makes Cottontale's render-on-demand budget a user-visible feature.

### What gets praised is time-to-table; what gets punished is both extremes

Praise concentrates on four mechanisms. Owlbear Rodeo needs no account or tutorial and drops the GM straight onto the tabletop ([StartPlaying](https://startplaying.games/blog/posts/a-comprehensive-list-of-virtual-tabletops)); its room link is shareable "in seconds" ([TTRPG Stack](https://www.ttrpgstack.com/best/virtual-tabletop/)). Shmeppy sells itself as "Your wet erase mat. Online." ([Shmeppy](https://shmeppy.com/about)). D&D Beyond ships Quickplay Maps with adventures, and TaleSpire's community closes its build-time gap with pasteable slabs and shared boards ([D&D Beyond, The Road Ahead](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2120-the-road-ahead-join-our-journey-to-improve-the); [TaleSpire on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/720620/TaleSpire/)). And actions hang on the selected thing: Foundry's Token HUD puts bars, status, elevation and the combat toggle one click from the token ([Foundry Tokens](https://foundryvtt.com/article/tokens/)).

The market punishes both ends. Full platforms draw "steep learning curve" and "time-intensive" scene setup ([TTRPG Stack](https://www.ttrpgstack.com/best/virtual-tabletop/)); minimal ones get called "a battlemap tool, not a full VTT" for lacking a tracker, HP and sheets ([StoryRoll](https://storyroll.app/blog/best-virtual-tabletops-dnd-2026), an interested party). The requests under D&D Beyond's own roadmap post, HP bars, status rings, notes, area-select fog, random names, read as the minimum "run a fight" kit a simple VTT is expected to carry built in ([D&D Beyond, The Road Ahead](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2120-the-road-ahead-join-our-journey-to-improve-the)). Cottontale has every item in that kit somewhere; the audit shows each one is three taps deep.

**Two vendors independently chose the same clutter remedy.** Owlbear Rodeo 2 replaced always-visible attachment handles with options that appear only when the token is selected, explicitly to cut clutter ([Owlbear Rodeo Dev Log 1](https://blog.owlbear.rodeo/owlbear-rodeo-2-0-dev-log-1/)), and Roll20's Jumpgate redesign of the context menu took "this opportunity to consider what options should be shown at what times" ([Roll20 blog, Jan 2024](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/roll20-jumpgate-redo-context-and-touch)). A third data point argues against Cottontale's current ring: Fantasy Grounds replaced its radial menu with a labelled list in v4.7.0, saying the radial "didn't have sufficient room for button labels" ([Fantasy Grounds v4.7.0](https://fantasygroundsunity.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/FGCP/pages/3077701648/v4.7.0+Feature+Showcase)). TaleSpire still uses a radial, so this is direction, not proof.

## Fast builders take one coarse gesture and derive the rest

### Cottontale already has the fast gestures; it lacks what comes after placement

The quickest map tools split labour the same way. Dungeon Alchemist's user picks a theme and drags; the app generates walls, floor, objects and lighting, then lets the user add, scale, rotate and remove ([Dungeon Alchemist on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1588530)). Townscaper allows only adding or removing a block and choosing its colour; its designer wants "the user to feel like they're in control of the large shapes" while details vary ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). Dungeon Scrawl's stamp tool scatters randomised copies along a drag, each staying individually editable ([Roll20 blog, May 2026](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/dungeon-scrawl-updates-may-2026/)). TaleSpire copies a dragged volume as a text slab ([TaleSpire on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/720620/TaleSpire/)).

Measured against that list, Cottontale is not behind. The audit records drag-to-paint a flat layer as one undo step, a room tool that builds floor, walls, windows and doorway from one drag, scatter, slab copy/paste shared as text, derived corner posts, and a generator with per-room refurnish and lock (audit §4, §7). The last is something Dungeon Alchemist's users are still requesting, in three separate threads asking to fix objects or lock walls before regenerating ([feature request](https://dungeonalchemist.featurebase.app/p/fix-objects-in-room-before-refreshing)). "Building is slow" therefore has a different cause, and the complaint record of the closest 3D analogue names it. TaleSpire's negative Steam reviews are about manipulating things after placement and about camera and precision, group move, cancel versus delete, tiles not landing where pointed, not about the placement brush (snippet-only, mixed dates: [TaleSpire Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/720620/reviews/?l=english)). Dungeon Alchemist users ask for drag-select and moving a room as a whole (snippet: [feature request](https://dungeonalchemist.upvoty.com/b/feature-requests/select-multiple-objects-by-dragging)). Cottontale's audit lists the same holes: a placed block or prop cannot be selected, moved, rotated or duplicated; rotation is set before placing by a chip that is usually scrolled off-screen; minis are placed in Build but named, statted and owned in Play (audit §4, §12 items 2 to 3).

### Two cautionary cases and one touch precedent

Dungeondraft's Building tool is the warning for any fast tool: its walls and floor cannot be touched by the precise Wall and Pattern tools, its floor re-randomises on every edit, and the community guide steers users away from it for any map over about ten minutes ([Dungeondraft Encyclopaedia](https://dungeondraft-encyclopaedia.gitbook.io/guide/all-the-tools/design-tab/building-tool)). Cottontale's room tool writes ordinary blocks and walls, which is the right side of that line and must stay so. The second warning is regeneration wider than intent, the root of the Dungeon Alchemist locking requests. Dungeon Scrawl's generator replaces the whole map after a warning ([Roll20 blog, May 2026](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/dungeon-scrawl-updates-may-2026/)); Cottontale's starter scene does worse today, writing straight to the store with no confirm and no single undo step (audit §4).

For a finger in 3D, Minecraft Bedrock is the reference, and it declines to choose: it ships tap-the-target and crosshair-with-buttons schemes side by side, a nine-slot hotbar as the working palette, and an optional on-screen "Pick Block" button ([Minecraft Wiki: Controls](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Controls)). TaleSpire's placement rule, snap to the hit surface by its normal, else climb on top, with a bounding volume shown before commit, is a ready-made answer to height without a height control ([TaleSpire Dev Log 214](https://bouncyrock.com/news/articles/talespire-dev-log-214)). The touch conventions for rotating are a rotate button in a bottom placement bar (The Sims Mobile) or tap-the-selected-thing (Happy Home Designer); both are snippet-only ([ProGameGuides](https://progameguides.com/the-sims-mobile/how-to-move-furniture-in-the-sims-mobile/); [Thonky](https://www.thonky.com/animal-crossing-happy-home-designer/home-design-guide)). Both avoid a twist gesture, which would collide with Cottontale's two-finger camera.

Generators expose one dominant action. Watabou's one-page dungeon makes a new map on Enter, rerolls notes separately from layout, and carries the seed in the link; on mobile, its author concedes, only "new map" is reachable (keys fetched: [Watabou devlog](https://watabou.itch.io/one-page-dungeon/devlog/491830/keyboard-shortcuts); mobile remark snippet-only). That sets the floor for a phone generator: one large reroll first, knobs behind it, reroll scoped by layer.

## Running a fight from a thumb needs a short list of large controls

### Damage entry is the primary action, and simpler trackers win

No source documents a phone-first GM surface; phone tooling is overwhelmingly player-side. What exists is a consistent shortlist of what small-screen tools bother to shortcut. Improved Initiative's headline is "you can apply damage or healing directly to the creature getting hit", yet the same review scores it 6/10 for ease of use against 9/10 for D&D Beyond's simpler tracker, whose stated flaw is that initiative order cannot be changed mid-encounter ([Arcane Eye](https://arcaneeye.com/dm-tools-5e/best-dnd-5e-initiative-tracker/)). Foundry's fastest damage flow is typing a signed number such as -7 into the token HUD ([Foundry Tokens](https://foundryvtt.com/article/tokens/)). D&D Beyond Maps adds every token on the map to initiative at once, and Roll20's Add Turn works on a multi-selection ([D&D Beyond, Navigating Maps](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1816-the-official-d-d-vtt-navigating-maps-on-d-d-beyond); [Roll20 blog, Jan 2024](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/roll20-jumpgate-redo-context-and-touch)). Swipe VTT keeps "volume, ruler, template and logout" always at hand plus a macro bar ([Swipe VTT](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/swipe-vtt)), and in-person GMs reach for a Stream Deck grid to "control the view, open sheets, trigger macros, and move tokens" ([Foundry Hub](https://foundryvtt-hub.com/guide/using-foundry-for-in-person-gaming)).

Cottontale's audit is the mirror image. HP moves in steps of one from the ring or by retyping a number; initiative is typed per mini, three taps deep, and typing it is what silently starts a fight; there is no start-fight, roll-all, reorder or combatant list; the turn strip and Next sit at the top of the screen; End fight clears every initiative in one unconfirmed tap; the soundboard is menu, sheet, scroll (audit §4, §12 items 14 to 20). The phone-tracker layout that sources support is a compact strip of turn cards at a screen edge ([Mobile Token Control](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/mobile-token-control)) plus a larger tracker opened on demand; a full-height list cannot share a phone with a map (inference).

### Movement, fog and dice each have a documented cheaper form

**Move and measure merged into one drag** in both Foundry v13 ("drag a token around the canvas and have the distance of that drag measured as you go", [Foundry 13.341](https://foundryvtt.com/releases/13.341)) and Owlbear Rodeo (snippet). Two vendors removing a tool switch from the most common live action is strong direction for Cottontale, where Move and Measure are separate dock slots.

Fog by brush is the documented slow path. D&D Beyond users say painting "slows play down a little having to get the right brush size and not accidentally reveal something" and ask for area select ([D&D Beyond, The Road Ahead](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2120-the-road-ahead-join-our-journey-to-improve-the)); on a phone browser its fog tools also lock out panning (snippet). Owlbear's prepared fog shapes make a room reveal a single toggle (snippet: [Owlbear docs](https://docs.owlbear.rodeo/docs/fog/)). Automatic vision fails at setup: fog "can be cool if the GM has everything setup correctly", which is "often not" ([EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/does-anybody-use-vtts-in-person.719680/)). Cottontale is well placed here, since line of sight derives from built walls and generated scenes already know their rooms; the audit's problems are that the Fog tool vanishes in free play and Reveal all is one unconfirmed tap (audit §8).

The Foundry touch grammar is tap to select, drag to move, double-tap for the sheet, long-press for the HUD, two fingers for the camera ([Swipe VTT](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/swipe-vtt)), which matches Cottontale's own except for double-tap. Three separate phone modules chose buttons or stepwise movement over free drag for players, and one later removed its gamepad ([Mobile Token Control](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/mobile-token-control); [Beaver's Mobile](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/beavers-mobile)), so drag is contested, not settled. On dice, the firm points are few: roll from the sheet line, offer a choice of where the roll happens, and let in-person groups use real dice ([Player Pilot](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/player-pilot)). Cottontale closing the sheet to roll from it (audit §12 item 19) runs against the first.

Hybrid play, a TV as the map and phones as controllers, is well documented and under-served: groups assemble it from five or six Foundry modules for a chrome-free, GM-steered, party-vision display ([Foundry Hub](https://foundryvtt-hub.com/guide/using-foundry-for-in-person-gaming)). No source covers a GM steering that display from a phone, which is exactly Cottontale's persona.

## Touch tools keep three things visible and swap the rest

Phone versions of serious creative tools do not shrink a toolbar. Procreate Pocket shows only the current tool, two side sliders and an expander; tapping the selected tool a second time opens its library ([Procreate Pocket: Interface](https://help.procreate.com/pocket/handbook/interface-gestures/interface)). Nomad Sculpt gives a user-configurable shortcut row with Undo and Redo, and a mirror for the other hand ([Nomad Sculpt manual](https://nomadsculpt.com/manual/interface)). CapCut and Canva swap the bottom bar to the selection's actions, with long-press as a second route to the same list (snippet-only: [Canva help](https://www.canva.com/help/navigate-canva-mobile-app/)). This is Cottontale's "replace, never stack" rule applied to selection, which the current chrome does not yet do: its Build dock holds five library categories rather than tools, and Erase and Undo sit at the top-right edge (audit §2).

On sheets, Apple and Nielsen Norman Group agree, and Cottontale already complies with most of it: one sheet at a time, a grabber that also works by tap, full-screen pages for long flows, nonmodal while the user works on the content behind ([Apple HIG: Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets); [NN/g: Bottom Sheets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/bottom-sheet/)). NN/g asks for two things beyond a grabber, a visible close button and system Back dismissing the sheet, and it calls the belief that bottom placement improves reachability "largely incorrect": the middle of the screen is the most accurately tapped. Hoober's field data adds that only 49% of observed use was one-handed, 36% cradled, and one-handed thumbs were 67% right and 33% left ([UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)). Two consequences follow for Cottontale. The top bar is the worst place for the mode switch, Next turn and Undo. And a one-handed GM needs a one-finger route to the camera in every mode, because two-finger gestures are natural only in the cradle grip; today, in every tool except Move, one finger never moves the camera (audit §7). The grip data is from 2013 phones and nothing found covers a hand that is also holding dice.

Gesture undo (two-finger tap, three-finger redo) is a convention in Procreate Pocket and Nomad, but each backs it with a visible button and an off switch, Pocket names the undone action in a notification, and Nomad users report accidental undos from a resting hand ([Procreate Pocket: Gestures](https://help.procreate.com/pocket/handbook/interface-gestures/gestures); forum report snippet-only). The rule that survives is that a gesture is an accelerator for something with a visible control. Roll20 tied undo to prep confidence directly: "The better our support for undo and redo is, the more confidently you can work on your game prep" ([Roll20 blog, Jan 2024](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/roll20-jumpgate-redo-context-and-touch)). In Cottontale, mini placement, moves and deletion, the starter scene, Reveal all and End fight all bypass undo (audit §8).

On onboarding, NN/g's test of 70 users found those who read an up-front tutorial rated tasks harder and were slower (snippet-only; [NN/g: Mobile tutorials](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)). The builders with the best first minute never show an empty canvas: Tiny Glade opens on themed glades with starting buildings ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)), Dungeon Scrawl generates a full dungeon with no account. World-building wikis give the same lesson for Prep in weaker evidence: LegendKeeper positions "no forms to fill out" against World Anvil's feature breadth (vendor claim, snippet), and an April 2026 field test credits templates with cutting setup time ([char-gen.com](https://char-gen.com/alternatives/world-anvil), a competitor's site).

## What Cottontale should adopt, in priority order

Priority reflects the owner's four pains weighted by evidence. "Board work" marks changes the audit says need `src/board.ts`, not only chrome; everything else is chrome plus an e2e rewrite, which is unavoidable in any re-layout (about 190 taps on 116 selectors, audit §11).

### Information architecture and modes

Make the dock the only navigation and let selection drive it. Play and Build stay the two table states, switched from the bottom, and entering Build keeps the selection, the open fight and the camera, as TaleSpire's build mode does. Selecting a mini or a placed piece replaces the dock with that thing's labelled actions and a More entry; the ring retires or survives only as an accelerator. Undo and Redo get a fixed, visible place in both modes. The 15-row menu dissolves: each feature gets one home, and settings and sound each get one screen. Role-gated tools show disabled with a reason instead of vanishing. Instructional feedback moves from the closed log to an on-table notice, the pattern Foundry v13 uses for a collapsed sidebar, "a minimalist chat notifications tray" ([Foundry 13.341](https://foundryvtt.com/releases/13.341)). Keep the sheet system as is, adding a close button and Back-to-dismiss on every sheet, and use the already-built, unused `pushSheetPage` for drill-downs.

### Build

First, add select-and-manipulate for placed blocks, props and minis: tap selects, the dock offers Move, Turn, Duplicate, Delete, and for minis name, HP and owner in place, ending the Build-then-Play double pass (board work). Second, give Build a one-finger camera route (board work; the form is an open decision). Third, turn the tray into a hotbar: recent and pinned pieces as the default row, tap-the-current-piece-again to open the library, a pick-from-scene button, and tool actions (Turn, Size, Repaint, Erase) separated from shelf selectors. Fourth, put every write on the undo stack, name the undone action in a toast, and replace confirm dialogs for table edits with undo. Fifth, make generation the default first act with one large reroll, layout and furnishing rerolled separately, user-edited pieces pinned automatically, and the whole result one undo step. Later: rooms as resizable objects with handles, a "category, varied member" slot in the manner of Canvas of Kings ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2498570/)), and a level stepper that says it caps placement.

### Play

First, build an encounter surface as a sheet: Start fight, add all or selected minis, roll all, drag to reorder, a combatant list with HP, and damage or heal entered as a number on a keypad and applied to one or several targets. The turn strip becomes a compact edge strip with Next turn in thumb reach, and End fight becomes undoable. Second, the selected-mini dock leads with HP, conditions and hide; Shoot, Shove and Emote move behind More. Third, a GM quick board, a Stream Deck-style grid for scene switch, soundboard, reveal and add-a-monster, reachable in one tap and not folded by a touch on the table. Fourth, show distance while dragging a mini, so Measure is needed only for templates. Fifth, reveal by room or region with one tap, keep the brush as the override, add a "see as players" check, and make Reveal all undoable. Sixth, roll from a sheet without closing it. A chrome-free table-display role for a TV is the largest optional bet.

### Finding things

One search that spans every category of piece is the minimum, since today a barrel cannot be found from Land or Figures (audit §3); extending it to actions and settings gives the long tail a single entry without new dock slots (inference only, no phone command-palette source was found). Re-cut categories by what the GM thinks of, ground, walls and doors, things, figures, with Scenes becoming generation and prefabs rather than a pseudo-category. Rename so "Import" and "Prep" each mean one thing.

### Onboarding

Split the lobby into "Start a table" and "Join", in plain words, with relays and keys behind an advanced fold. The creator becomes GM by default, as in Shmeppy where the creator of a game is its GM (snippet: [Shmeppy forum](https://forum.shmeppy.com/t/getting-started-with-shmeppy/1719)). A new table opens on a themed, generated scene with reroll visible, not an empty board with a four-verb card. A joining player is taken to their mini or asked to claim one. Teach each gesture once, in context, one hint at a time; no tour.

**Patterns to avoid**, each with a named precedent: fast tools whose output precise tools cannot edit (Dungeondraft); regeneration that overwrites manual work (Dungeon Alchemist); a tool that captures every touch and blocks the camera (D&D Beyond Maps fog); icon-only radials (Fantasy Grounds); features reachable only by a modifier or hidden gesture (Foundry's modifier-key set; Owlbear designed its ruler option without one "for mobile"); up-front tutorials and coach-mark chains (NN/g); stacked sheets; long-press as the delete path, which Minecraft slows deliberately; and relying on contact-area palm rejection, which Nomad's own manual says does not work on every device.

## Decisions that need the owner

| # | Decision | Options and what bears on it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is Prep a mode or a destination? | Today it is a third "mode" that never shows as on. A single "Table" slot opening full-screen pages (absorbing the menu's boards, export, invite) is cleaner; keeping three modes preserves the current mental model. |
| 2 | One-finger camera in Build | A hand/camera slot in the dock; drag on empty space pans while drag on a surface paints; or a user-chosen scheme as Minecraft offers. Each changes what "one finger = the current tool" means. |
| 3 | Does Build default to Select or to the last brush? | Select-first suits fixing and furnishing; brush-first suits terrain. Determines how much of `board.ts`'s brush model is reworked. |
| 4 | Fate of the ring | Replace with a dock swap, or keep it as an on-piece accelerator beside the dock. The only direct evidence is Fantasy Grounds; TaleSpire keeps a radial. |
| 5 | Creator-is-GM and free play | Making the creator GM fixes the role moment and hidden Fog, but ends "everyone can build" as the default. Is free play kept as an explicit choice? |
| 6 | Two-finger tap undo | It would be the one exception to "a second finger must never cause an edit". Ship optional, or not at all. |
| 7 | Encounter tracking with no rules system | Should HP, initiative and conditions work generically when "No rules" is picked, and how much does the tracker defer to a system's schema? |
| 8 | Fog model | Room/region toggles first with brush as override, or line of sight as the default with manual fog secondary. |
| 9 | Player movement | Keep free drag, or add tap-destination with a path preview. Sources show the contest but no winner. |
| 10 | TV table-display role | In scope for this redesign or deferred. The need is documented; the GM-phone-plus-TV combination has no prior art. |
| 11 | Clean slate or staged | A full re-layout rewrites most of the phone e2e section at once; staging (selection dock, then encounter sheet, then lobby) keeps checks green. Foundry's v13 overhaul drew a "Classic UI" restore module, though Cottontale has no UI extension ecosystem to break. |
| 12 | Handedness and the dock's six slots | Whether to ship a left-hand mirror, and which five or six slots each mode gets once Undo, mode switch and a quick board compete for them. |

## Conclusion

The research changes where the redesign's effort belongs. Going in, "building is slow" suggested better build tools; the comparison shows Cottontale's gestures already match or lead Dungeon Alchemist, Townscaper and TaleSpire, and that its slowness lives in the seams, between placing and adjusting, between Build and Play, between the thumb and the top of the screen. One structural move, letting the selected thing decide what the dock shows, addresses three of the four pains at once and is the best-evidenced pattern in the whole survey.

The absence of a phone-GM incumbent cuts both ways. There is nothing to imitate and nothing validated, so the riskiest choices here, one-finger camera, select-first Build, the encounter sheet's layout, should be settled by prototypes on a 390x844 viewport with a real session, not by more desk research. The gaps the researchers hit, Reddit, hands-on tracker layouts, Owlbear on phones, are narrow enough that an hour with each app would close them faster than another search pass.
